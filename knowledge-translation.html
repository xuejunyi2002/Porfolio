About the Project

Project Introduction

Knowledge Translation is an AI powered web platform created in collaboration with UBC Library to make academic research easier to understand and access.

The platform transforms complex research into accessible formats, including text summaries, podcasts, infographics, and conversational interaction. As Co Project Manager and UI Sub Team Lead, I supported the overall production process while leading the design workflow for the user facing platform.

Key Challenges

Problem → Response → Outcome

Knowledge Translation brought together a large cross functional student team to build an AI platform within a short academic timeline. As Co Project Manager and UI Sub Team Lead, my focus was to maintain visibility across workstreams, connect sub team progress to shared milestones, and translate broad stakeholder goals into achievable production priorities.

Challenge
My Response
Outcome
01

Coordinating a Large Cross Functional Team

The project included multiple workstreams across UI, development, AI integration, research, content, and stakeholder communication. Each sub team worked at a different pace, creating risks around unclear ownership, hidden dependencies, duplicated effort, and disconnected progress.

I co managed the project through a shared Scrum workflow in Notion, with visible ownership, weekly priorities, milestones, and action items. Regular meetings and written updates helped connect sub teams, surface blockers, and clarify which tasks depended on work from other disciplines.

The team gained a clearer shared view of progress, responsibilities, and dependencies. Sub teams could work with greater independence while remaining connected to the overall project direction and delivery timeline.

02

Connecting Sub Team Progress to Overall Delivery

As both Co Project Manager and UI Sub Team Lead, I needed to support detailed UI production without allowing the design workstream to become disconnected from development, AI integration, content readiness, or the broader project schedule. Progress within one sub team did not always mean a feature was ready for implementation.

I connected UI tasks to shared project milestones and tracked dependencies beyond the design team. I coordinated review and handoff points, clarified what information developers needed before implementation, and adjusted UI priorities when dependent systems or content were not yet ready. This kept local design decisions aligned with broader production needs.

The UI team could continue moving forward without producing work isolated from implementation. Design, content, and development handoffs became clearer, and the final interface remained more closely aligned with what the full team could realistically deliver.

03

Turning Broad Feedback into Clear Priorities

Stakeholder and internal feedback covered accessibility, navigation, content presentation, feature clarity, and technical feasibility. Within a short timeline, the team could not treat every suggestion as an immediate production requirement.

I helped organize feedback into themes and evaluate it through user value, urgency, effort, dependencies, and alignment with the project goal. Important changes became specific design or production tasks, while lower priority ideas were documented without disrupting active work.

The team responded to meaningful feedback while protecting the delivery timeline. Iteration became more focused, and decisions remained connected to shared project priorities rather than isolated opinions.

Leadership Principles

What I Carry Forward

01

Visibility Connects Independent Teams

Cross functional teams need enough autonomy to move efficiently, but they also need a shared view of priorities, ownership, and dependencies. I carry forward a transparent production approach that allows teams to work independently without losing alignment.

02

Local Progress Must Support Shared Delivery

Sub team progress creates value only when it connects to the wider production plan. I carry forward an approach that links local tasks, review points, and handoffs to shared milestones so that work remains useful, implementable, and aligned with delivery.

03

Feedback Needs a Decision Framework

Stakeholder feedback creates value when it is evaluated against user needs, effort, dependencies, and project goals. I carry forward a structured approach that turns broad feedback into focused and achievable production decisions.
